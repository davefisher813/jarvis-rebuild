The grey scale was never measured.

A craft audit of JARVIS — type, color, containers, buttons, spacing, hierarchy — measured from computed style on the rendered page rather than read out of the stylesheet. It opens with a bug in the tool that was supposed to be checking this.

30 pages walked 390×844 · both themes 24 type pairs painted 96 red rules

The auditor was scoring transparent text as opaque

Yesterday's contrast pass reported zero findings. That number was an artifact of a one-line bug, not a clean bill of health.

The rel() helper in visual-audit.mjs matched all the digits in a color string and used the first three. Given rgba(235, 235, 245, 0.3) it measured solid #EBEBF5 — and every --tx-* grey in JARVIS is rgba. The entire secondary and tertiary text layer was checked as if it were pure white.

2.25Nav labels

Your inactive tab labels — Tasks, Schedule, More — render at 2.25:1. That is the app's primary navigation at half the AA threshold, and below even the 3:1 floor for non-text. Apple's own inactive tab grey sits at 4.6:1.

Live swatches — those are the real alpha values over the real surfaces.

Root cause: one grey scale was authored once and reused across two themes whose surfaces have opposite luminance. Dark got away with it at tx-3; light did not. The alphas that hold on every surface in their own theme:

[data-theme="light"] { --tx-3: rgba(60,60,67,0.75);   /* was .60 */
                       --tx-4: rgba(60,60,67,0.75); }
[data-theme="dark"]  { --tx-3: rgba(235,235,245,0.60); /* already ok */
                       --tx-4: rgba(235,235,245,0.60); }

Not applied. This is a design decision, not a bug fix — see below.

Bringing tx-4 up to AA means it stops being a third grey; it collapses into tx-3 and you lose a tier of visual quiet. The alternative is to keep tx-4 dim and put only decoration in it, never words — which means the inactive tab labels have to move to tx-3 regardless. Your call, so I left the tokens alone.

Three things to fix

Two screen titles, two pixels apart, both weight 800. .pagehead-title is a hardcoded 34px; .today-title is 32px from the token. Nobody can perceive that as intentional — pick one.

Three eyebrow specs for one role. On Today you can see 12/800 "Heads Up" sitting directly above 11/800 "Your Day, Drafted", with 11/700 "Events" elsewhere. Same job, three treatments.

Five weights at 15px. Five near-identical treatments at one size isn't a hierarchy — the reader can't decode it. 15/500 is used by exactly one class, which means it was typed, not chosen. Same for 14/400, 21px, 19px, 26px and 27px.

A closed set that covers everything above: 32/800 title · 20/700 section · 17/400 row · 17/700 button · 15/400 meta · 15/700 emphasis · 11/700 eyebrow · 10/600 tab. Eight pairs, down from twenty-four.

Containers

Two unreconciled ideas of what a list is, and fourteen pixels of rag inside a single card.

Today wraps everything in .card on a raised surface. Tasks and Schedule put rows directly on the page background with a hairline divider. Same app, same row content, two different container languages — and they're the three most-used screens.

Ragged text rails inside one list. On Money, "Rent" and "Car Insurance" start at x=72 while "Internet" starts at x=58 — because Internet has a 32px square icon and the others have a 26px circle. The icon slot needs a fixed width so the text shares one rail.

Hierarchy

Dominance is the area of the largest element on a screen divided by the second largest. A page with a focal point scores well above 1. Six of your pages score exactly 1.00.

PageDominanceReading
Brain · Notes · More · Settings · Decisions · Connections1.00perfectly flat — every row identical weight, no entry point
Tasks1.07the red button barely out-weighs the title
Your Routine · Account · AI Control1.09–1.17flat

Those pages are navigable, but nothing tells the eye where to start. The counter-example is already in the app: Focus / Up Next is one card, one sentence of reason, and two buttons. It has an obvious focal point and nothing else competing. It is the best-designed screen you have — the rest of the app should be argued against it.

Where minimalism slips

Tasks stacks two horizontal scrollers back to back — filter chips, then category chips — directly under two competing primary buttons. Four rows of chrome before the first task, and both strips clip mid-word at the right edge ("Overdu…"), which reads as broken rather than scrollable.

Today renders five sections plus two persistent bars above the fold.

Dead space that isn't working: Appearance is one segmented control and ~1,300px of black. Connections is a ~1,100px empty card. Person detail is one row then ~900px. Money's balance card is ~250px tall for three lines with the entire right half empty.

What's genuinely good

  • The 16px page rail is consistent across all thirty pages.
  • Zero nested cards anywhere — hard to hold onto, and you have.
  • The surface set is tight and correct — real iOS values, no drift.
  • The pill radius family is used consistently for every pill-shaped thing.
  • Focus / Up Next is a properly designed screen: one focal point, two choices, nothing else.
  • The time ring — 1h 30m over OPEN — is the strongest component in the app. Continuous, glanceable, and yours.
